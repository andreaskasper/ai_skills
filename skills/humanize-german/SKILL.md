# Humanize German: writing German without AI patterns

These rules make German texts read as natural, factual and trustworthy, without the features by which readers (and Wikipedia editors) recognise AI-generated content. The yardstick is the German Wikipedia ideal: sober, restrained, sourced.

Sources: the community collections [Anzeichen für KI-generierte Inhalte](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Anzeichen_f%C3%BCr_KI-generierte_Inhalte) (German) and [Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing) (English). The English page is more detailed; its observations are transferred to German here.

These are not bans on single words but hints at recurring patterns. Each has a reason; understand the reason and decide case by case. No single feature proves machine origin. Accumulation is what's suspicious. Conversely, avoiding every pattern doesn't make a text good; it also needs substance (concrete facts, real examples, verifiable sources).

The examples below are German on purpose: they are the phrases to avoid or prefer in the output.

## Basic attitude

Write like an informed, reserved person: clear, concrete, without staging yourself or the topic. Say what is, not how significant it is. If a sentence adds nothing but solemnity, delete it.

Self-test: could the sentence appear in an advertising brochure or a scientific abstract? Then it is probably too inflated for a factual German text.

## Sources and facts

Never invent sources, titles, authors, years, links, ISBNs or DOIs. This is the most important rule.

Cite only sources whose existence can be checked, and only for statements they actually contain. If something is uncertain, mark it as such or leave it out: a gap is better than invented certainty.

A subtler pattern: the source exists but doesn't support the statement. A valid DOI leading to a different paper, a real book on a related but wrong topic, a book citation without page number, a link to a search results page instead of the document, a reference in the bibliography that no statement points to. Check not only that a source exists, but that it says exactly what you attribute to it.

Treat an unsourced claim as a possible error, not a fact. When in doubt, phrase cautiously rather than confidently wrong.

## Language and tone

**No inflated significance.** The topic doesn't need to justify its importance constantly. Avoid „spielt eine bedeutende Rolle“, „unterstreicht die Bedeutung“, „steht als Zeugnis für“, „gilt als Wendepunkt“, „hinterlässt einen bleibenden Eindruck“, „tief verwurzelt“, „unerschütterlich“. State what concretely happened or applies.

**No promotional language.** „reiches kulturelles Erbe“, „atemberaubend“, „beeindruckende Schönheit“, „bleibendes Vermächtnis“, „im Herzen von“, „unbedingt sehen“ belong in brochures. Stay neutral, also on topics that are easily charged emotionally (places, culture, people, species).

**No editorial self-commentary**: „es ist wichtig zu beachten“, „es ist bemerkenswert, dass“, „erwähnenswert ist“, „keine Betrachtung wäre vollständig ohne“. If something matters, the content shows it.

**No opinion presented as fact.** If an assessment is needed, attribute it.

**Don't prove notability by assertion.** AI texts like to make media coverage the content: „wurde in überregionalen Medien besprochen“, „fand Beachtung in der Fachpresse“, „unterhält eine aktive Präsenz in sozialen Medien“, or listing source types („unabhängige Berichterstattung“, „regionale Medien“). Write what happened and put the source where sources go, not into the running text as an argument.

**No vague authorities / weasel words**: „Beobachter meinen“, „Kritiker argumentieren“, „Branchenberichte zeigen“, „es wird oft gesagt“, „Studien zeigen“. Name who says it, or drop the claim. Don't extend one source's view to a large, vague group.

**Avoid AI buzzwords** where a plainer word says the same: „eintauchen“, „beleuchten“, „lebendig“, „facettenreich“, „nahtlos“, „robust“, „entscheidend“/„zentral“ as constant emphasis, „spannend“ as filler, „Landschaft“ as metaphor („die digitale Landschaft“). Likewise pseudo-scientific inflation („kausal“, „empirisch“, „korreliert“) without real data.

## Sentences and wording

**Dashes sparingly.** Clustered dashes, especially where a comma, colon or parenthesis would be more natural, are now the best-known AI signal. One deliberate dash is fine; several per paragraph are a warning sign. For ranges use the en dash without spaces: „2020–2024“, not „2020-2024“.

**Connectives sparingly and naturally.** „Darüber hinaus“, „zusätzlich“, „außerdem“, „ferner“, „andererseits“ get chained mechanically in AI texts. An occasional connective is good; every paragraph starting with one is a warning sign.

**No formulaic negative parallelism**: „nicht nur …, sondern auch …“, „es geht nicht nur um …, sondern“, „es ist kein …, sondern ein …“, and „X statt Y“ as a constant figure. It sounds argumentative and promotional.

**Don't shy away from plain copula sentences.** AI avoids „ist/sind/hat“ systematically: „dient als“, „fungiert als“, „stellt dar“, „markiert“, „bietet“, „verfügt über“. „Das Gebäude ist das Ausstellungshaus“ beats „Das Gebäude fungiert als Ausstellungshaus“. Same for „es gibt“, „es hat“.

**Plain verb over stiff synonym**: „schrieb“ not „verfasste“, „starb“ not „verstarb“, „zog um“ not „siedelte über“, „nutzte“ not „bediente sich“, „hielt für“ not „erachtete“, „half“ not „leistete Unterstützung“.

**No forced synonym rotation** („die Stadt … die Metropole … der urbane Raum“). Repeating the right word is fine.

**No rhetorical triads** („schnell, zuverlässig und elegant“, „sowohl … als auch … und“).

**No trailing participle interpretations**: „…, wodurch die Bedeutung unterstrichen wird“, „…, was seine Rolle hervorhebt“, „gewährleistend“, „widerspiegelnd“. Write a separate sentence or drop the interpretation.

**No fake ranges** with „von … bis“ that merely string examples together.

**Don't sand off what makes texts human.** Hedges („vielleicht“, „eher“, „ziemlich“, „sehr“, „meist“), clear superlatives („der erste“, „der einzige“, „einer der größten“) and slightly clunky but common constructions („um … zu“, „infolge“, „die Tatsache, dass“) are normal German. Keep them where they're true and fit.

## Structure

**Be brief.** AI texts are often too long and too structured. Not every thought needs a heading; short answers usually work as plain prose.

**No closing „Fazit“** and no final paragraph that just repeats. If a summary is needed, it goes at the start.

**No schematic „Herausforderungen und Ausblick“ block** („Trotz seiner Erfolge steht … vor mehreren Herausforderungen“, „Zukunftsaussichten“, „Vermächtnis“). Mention concrete problems where they belong in the content.

**No marketing-style (often two-part) subheadings.** Headings name, they don't advertise.

## Formatting

- **Bold sparingly**: at most single, truly central terms.
- **Lists only for real lists.** Connected reasoning belongs in sentences. Use the target system's list formatting, not pasted •, - or – characters.
- **No "bullet + bold keyword + colon + explanation"** pattern („**Flexibilität:** Das System erlaubt …“). Also no mini tables for content that fits in a sentence.
- **Keep the heading hierarchy**; don't skip levels.
- **German capitalisation in headings**, not English title case: „Die wichtigsten Vorteile im Überblick“, not „Die Wichtigsten Vorteile Im Überblick“.
- **No emojis** before headings or bullets, and none in factual texts unless requested.
- **No decorative separator lines.**

## Technical artefacts

- No Markdown leftovers (`**bold**`, `###`, backticks, pasted list markers) in places that don't render Markdown.
- Remove chatbot citation leftovers completely: URLs with `utm_source=chatgpt.com`, fragments like „oaicite“, „:contentReference“, „turn0search“ (ChatGPT), „[cite: 1]“, „[span_1]“ (Gemini), „grok_render_citation_card“ (Grok). Check copied links exist and lead to the stated content.
- Consistent quotation marks, in German „so“; don't mix straight and typographic quotes.
- No truncated sentences or abruptly ending texts.

## No dialogue or meta leftovers

Write the finished text, not the conversation about it:
- No „Ich hoffe, das hilft“, „Gerne!“, „Natürlich!“, „Gute Frage!“, „Hier ist der Artikel“, „Möchtest du, dass ich …“, no greetings in running texts or discussion posts.
- No letter formulas in non-letters („Ich hoffe, diese Nachricht erreicht Sie wohlbehalten“).
- No self-description of the draft („Dieser Text wahrt einen neutralen Ton“).
- No remarks on your own knowledge cutoff („Stand meines letzten Updates“, „soweit in den verfügbaren Quellen ersichtlich“). State uncertainty about the matter itself.
- No leftover placeholders („[Name einfügen]“, „hier Details ergänzen“).

## What is NOT a tell-tale sign

Avoiding these makes a text worse without making it more human:

- Flawless spelling and grammar. Inserting mistakes doesn't make a text more real.
- A formal, academic or sober tone. Only certain words are AI-typical, not elevated language as such.
- A single connective. Only mechanical accumulation stands out.
- Switching between factual and casual register.
- Missing citations: a content problem, not a sign of machine origin.

The reverse doesn't hold either: a text without these patterns can be machine-made, a text with some of them human.

## Quick self-check before sending

- Are all sources and facts real and verifiable, and does each source support the statement it's attached to?
- Any promotional or significance language that can go?
- A „Fazit“ or „Herausforderungen“ block or a closing repetition?
- Paragraphs mechanically starting with „Darüber hinaus“, „Zusätzlich“ etc.?
- Dashes piling up where comma or colon would do?
- Avoiding plain „ist/hat“ („dient als“, „fungiert als“), stiff synonyms („verfasste“, „verstarb“) or forced synonym rotation?
- Significance claimed via media presence instead of content?
- Too much bold, too many headings, too many lists?
- Technical artefacts (Markdown leftovers, citation leftovers, mixed quotes)?
- Dialogue or meta leftovers?

If any answer is "yes" (or "no" for the first): revise, but selectively. Not every formal sentence and not every „vielleicht“ is a mistake; see "What is NOT a tell-tale sign".
