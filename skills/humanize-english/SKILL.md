# Humanize English: writing without AI patterns

These rules make English texts read as natural, factual and trustworthy, without the features by which readers and Wikipedia editors recognise AI-generated content. The yardstick is encyclopedic prose: plain, restrained, sourced.

Source: the community guide [Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing). A German counterpart of this skill is `vermenschlichen`.

These are patterns, not banned words. Each has a reason; understand it and decide case by case. No single feature proves machine origin; accumulation is what stands out. And avoiding every pattern doesn't make a text good: it also needs substance (concrete facts, real examples, verifiable sources).

## Basic attitude

Write like an informed, understated person: clear, concrete, not performing importance. Say what is, not how significant it is. If a sentence adds nothing but solemnity, cut it.

Self-test: would the sentence fit in a tourism brochure or a grant proposal? Then it's probably too inflated for factual prose.

## Sources and facts

Never invent sources, titles, authors, dates, URLs, ISBNs or DOIs. This is the most important rule.

Cite only sources you can verify, and only for claims they actually make. If something is uncertain, say so or leave it out.

The subtler failure: the source exists but doesn't support the claim. A valid DOI pointing to a different paper, a real book on a neighbouring topic, a book citation without a page number, a link to a search results page, a reference that no statement relies on. Check that the source says exactly what you attribute to it.

Treat an unsourced claim as a possible error. Prefer a hedged true statement to a confident false one.

## Language and tone

**No inflated significance.** Avoid "plays a pivotal/crucial/vital role", "stands as a testament to", "underscores the importance of", "marks a turning point", "leaves a lasting legacy", "deeply rooted", "enduring", "indelible mark". State what happened.

**No promotional language.** "rich cultural heritage", "breathtaking", "stunning", "nestled in the heart of", "vibrant community", "must-visit", "boasts" belong in ads. Stay neutral, especially on places, cultures, people and species.

**No editorial signposting**: "It's important to note that", "It is worth mentioning", "Notably,", "Interestingly,", "No discussion of X would be complete without". If something matters, the content shows it.

**No opinion as fact.** Attribute assessments to whoever holds them.

**Don't argue notability through coverage.** "has been featured in major publications", "has garnered significant media attention", "maintains an active social media presence". Say what happened; put sources where sources go.

**No vague attributions / weasel words**: "experts say", "critics argue", "industry reports suggest", "many believe", "studies show". Name who, or drop it. Don't inflate one source into "observers".

**Avoid AI vocabulary** where a plainer word works: "delve", "tapestry", "testament", "landscape" (as metaphor), "realm", "navigate" (challenges), "foster", "leverage", "harness", "robust", "seamless", "multifaceted", "intricate", "nuanced", "showcase", "underscore", "bolster", "elevate", "unlock", "embark", "game-changer", "in today's fast-paced world". Also pseudo-scientific inflation ("causal", "empirical", "correlates") without data behind it.

## Sentences

**Em dashes sparingly.** Frequent em dashes where a comma, colon or parentheses would do are now the best-known AI tell. One deliberate dash is fine; several per paragraph are a warning sign.

**Transitions sparingly.** "Moreover", "Furthermore", "Additionally", "In addition", "However", "Consequently" opening sentence after sentence is mechanical.

**No formulaic parallelism**: "not only … but also", "It's not just X, it's Y", "This isn't about X — it's about Y", "rather than X, Y" as a recurring figure.

**Use plain copulas.** AI avoids "is/are/has": "serves as", "stands as", "functions as", "represents", "features", "boasts", "offers". "The building is the museum's main hall" beats "The building serves as the museum's main hall".

**No elegant variation.** Don't rotate synonyms to avoid repetition ("the city … the metropolis … the urban centre"). Repeat the right word.

**No rule of three** as reflex: "fast, reliable, and elegant", three parallel clauses for rhythm.

**No superficial -ing tails**: "…, highlighting its importance", "…, underscoring the need for", "…, reflecting the community's resilience", "…, ensuring that". Write a separate sentence with actual content, or cut.

**No false ranges**: "from ancient rituals to modern festivals" that only strings examples together.

**Don't sand off what makes text human.** Hedges ("probably", "fairly", "mostly"), clear superlatives ("the first", "the only", "one of the largest") and ordinary slightly clunky phrasing are normal. Keep them where they are true.

## Structure

- **Be brief.** Not every thought needs a heading; short answers work as prose.
- **No closing "Conclusion"/"In summary"/"Overall"** that repeats the text. If a summary is needed, put it first.
- **No "Challenges and future prospects" / "Legacy" block** ("Despite its success, X faces several challenges…"). Mention real problems where they belong.
- **No marketing headings** ("Unlocking the Power of X: A Deep Dive").

## Formatting

- **Bold sparingly**, only truly central terms.
- **Lists only for real lists.** Reasoning belongs in sentences.
- **No "bullet + bold label + colon + explanation"** list ("**Flexibility:** The system allows…") for content that is really prose. No tiny tables for what fits in a sentence.
- **Keep heading levels consistent**; in encyclopedic or documentation contexts prefer sentence case ("History of the site", not "History Of The Site") unless the house style says otherwise.
- **No emojis** before headings or bullets in factual text unless asked.
- **No decorative horizontal rules** between every section.

## Technical artefacts

- No Markdown leftovers (`**`, `###`, backticks) where Markdown doesn't render.
- Remove chatbot citation debris completely: `utm_source=chatgpt.com`, "oaicite", ":contentReference", "turn0search" (ChatGPT), "[cite: 1]", "[span_1]" (Gemini), "grok_render_citation_card" (Grok). Check that copied links exist and lead to the stated content.
- Consistent quotation marks and apostrophes; don't mix straight and curly within a text.
- No truncated sentences or abrupt endings.

## No dialogue or meta leftovers

Write the finished text, not the conversation about it:
- No "Certainly!", "Great question!", "Here is the article:", "I hope this helps", "Let me know if you'd like…", "Would you like me to…".
- No letter formulas in non-letters ("I hope this message finds you well").
- No self-description ("This article maintains a neutral tone").
- No knowledge-cutoff remarks ("As of my last update", "based on available information"). State uncertainty about the subject itself.
- No placeholders ("[Insert name]", "add details here").

## What is NOT a tell-tale sign

Avoiding these makes a text worse, not more human:
- Correct spelling and grammar. Adding mistakes doesn't make text authentic.
- A formal or academic register as such.
- A single transition word or a single em dash.
- Mixing formal and casual registers.
- Missing citations: a content problem, not a sign of AI.

And the reverse: text without these patterns can be machine-made; text with some of them can be human.

## Quick self-check before sending

- Are all sources and facts real, and does each source support its claim?
- Any significance or promotional language to cut?
- A "Conclusion" or "Challenges" block, or a repeating last paragraph?
- Sentences mechanically opening with "Moreover", "Additionally"?
- Em dashes piling up?
- "serves as"/"boasts" instead of "is"/"has", synonym rotation, reflexive triads, -ing tails?
- Notability argued through media coverage?
- Too much bold, too many headings or lists?
- Markdown or citation artefacts, mixed quotes?
- Dialogue or meta leftovers?

If any answer is "yes" (or "no" for the first), revise selectively. Not every formal sentence or hedge is a problem; see "What is NOT a tell-tale sign".
